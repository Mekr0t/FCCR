# S2-4 truncation ablation (dev only)

Issue #10. Model mE5-large (fp16, 512 tokens), reference runs `dense_mono_dev_both` (mono) and `dense_cross_dev_eng` (cross; `dense_cross_dev_both` and the single-variant runs are listed too).
Overall = official headline (mono: macro over languages; cross: micro). Affected / unaffected subsets = micro over posts of the subset, also for mono.
Affected = posts whose `both` text exceeds 512 tokens; the count is ntok in `both_posts/ntok.npy`, i.e. tokens of `query: ` + text with `<s>`/`</s>` included, before truncation (the same definition the runner uses).
Variant A scores cosine(post_orig, fc_orig) and cosine(post_eng, fc_eng) over the full pool (RRF: ranks of the two full rankings, k=60, equal weights; 3-way mean adds the `both` score).
Variant B cuts `both` posts above 512 tokens into 450-token windows with 50-token overlap on raw token ids (no regard for the orig/eng boundary), embeds each window with `query: `, and scores against the existing `both` fact-check embeddings (max over windows, or mean window embedding re-normalised); other posts keep their `both` embedding. Dev: 633 windows from 219 mono posts, 172 windows from 56 cross posts, all at most 456 tokens including prefix and special tokens; embedding took about 6 s on the RTX 5070 per track. No fact-check was re-embedded.
Selection and reporting are on the same dev set, so the best-variant deltas are optimistic. Affected subsets are small (219 and 56 posts); differences of a few points there are within noise (one post in the cross subset is 1.8 points).

## Interpretation

Chunking with max over windows helps exactly where it should: on the 219 truncated mono posts S@10 rises from 0.8037 to 0.8311, and unaffected posts are unchanged by construction. Overall this is only +0.0012 S@10 (and -0.0010 MRR) because the affected posts are 11.6% of the data and per-language changes are mixed (deu -0.012, eng -0.002, fra/por/spa +0.007 to +0.011), so on mono the result is a negligible gain that we would not call significant. In the crosslingual track the `both` < `eng` gap (0.5326 vs 0.5906) is mostly not a post-truncation effect: unaffected posts show nearly the same gap (0.5403 vs 0.5968), and chunking `both` recovers only 0.5326 to 0.5362 overall (2 of 56 affected posts). Hypothesis, not tested here: the gap comes from concatenating the original and the English text into one embedding (dilution, or cut-off of long fact-check texts on the pool side, which this issue did not touch), not from the truncated tail of long posts. The largest gain is variant A on the crosslingual track: the mean of the orig and eng cosine scores reaches 0.6467 (+0.0562 S@10, +0.0446 MRR vs `dense_cross_dev_eng`), also on unaffected posts (0.6532 vs 0.5968), so it acts as a two-view ensemble rather than a truncation fix; max is worse than mean, and RRF in between (hypothesis: cosine magnitudes of the two views are comparable, so averaging keeps information that rank-only fusion discards). On mono, separate orig+eng mean (0.8542) stays below the single `both` embedding (0.8617), and only the 3-way mean (0.8625) matches it. Net: truncation of long posts costs little (about 0.3 points overall on mono at best), while score-level combination of views is worth testing further for the crosslingual track (selection on dev only; the final test run, if any, belongs to the fusion/final-run issues).
